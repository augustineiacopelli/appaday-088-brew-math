How to use it & get correct numbers ▼

Hydrometer mode (specific gravity)

Type each reading as a full specific gravity value, digits and all: 1.052 for the original, 1.010 for the final. Do not enter just the gravity points (52 or 10) or a Plato number, the math depends on the 1.0xx form.

  • Temperature-correct first. Hydrometers are calibrated at one temperature, usually 60°F / 20°C. A warm sample reads low. Adjust to the calibration temperature before entering, or your ABV will drift.
  • Read at eye level, at the bottom of the meniscus, with no clinging bubbles on the stem.
  • The final gravity must be lower than the original. If it is not, fermentation is unfinished or a reading is off.

Refractometer mode (Brix)

Enter both readings in degrees Brix (°Bx) exactly as they appear in the eyepiece. Two things make refractometer results honest:

  • Set your Wort Correction Factor. Refractometers are calibrated for sugar water, and wort reads slightly high. Dividing by the WCF converts the reading to true Brix. The quickest way to get yours: tap Calibrate, enter one refractometer reading alongside the temperature-corrected hydrometer OG from the same pre-yeast sample, and it solves the factor and fills it in. If you have never calibrated, leave it at 1.04.
  • The alcohol correction is automatic. Once fermentation starts, alcohol bends light and a refractometer can no longer read gravity directly. This app runs the Terrill cubic correction on your final Brix to recover the true final gravity, then does the rest of the math on that. The corrected OG and FG are shown under the inputs so you can sanity-check them.
  • Take readings near room temperature. Automatic-temperature-compensating (ATC) units handle small swings on their own.

What each number means

  • ABV is alcohol by volume, the headline strength of the beer.
  • Apparent attenuation is how much of the measured gravity the yeast consumed. It reads high because alcohol is lighter than water and drags the final gravity down.
  • Real attenuation backs the alcohol out and reflects the true fraction of sugar fermented. It is always the smaller, more honest figure.
  • Calories combine alcohol and residual sugar, shown per 12 oz and per 16 oz (US pint).

Formulas used

ABV = (76.08 × (OG − FG) / (1.775 − OG)) × (FG / 0.794)

Apparent att. = (OG − FG) / (OG − 1) × 100

Real att. = (OE − RE) / OE × 100,  RE = 0.1808×OE + 0.8192×AE

Cal (12 oz) = alcohol + residual-sugar calories from OG & FG

OE and AE are the original and apparent extract in degrees Plato, from a standard specific-gravity-to-Plato polynomial. Brix to gravity uses the sucrose refractive-index relation.

Results are close estimates for planning and label copy, not lab measurements. Attenuation and calories assume normal wort; unusual adjuncts will shift the real figures.
